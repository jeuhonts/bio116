BIO 116 · Bioinformatics

One gene, four labs

These four interactive labs follow a single piece of human DNA: 10,309 bases of chromosome 11 around HRAS, the first human cancer gene ever identified. You sequence and assemble it, find the gene in it, read it in a genome browser, and finally annotate it for other scientists to use. Do the labs in order, because each one builds on the last.

How to work

Each lab has sections with an interactive panel beside the reading. Checks under each section tell you straight away whether you are right, with a hint if not. The progress bar at the top of each lab tracks what you have finished.

Your progress

Progress is saved in this browser, on this device only. It is not sent anywhere, and it is not your grade. Use the same computer and browser to pick up where you left off.

The graded exercise

Lab 3 ends with a personal set of questions. Type your answers (pasting is turned off), press Submit and save file, and upload the file it saves to the assignment on the course site. The practice checks in every lab are not graded.

Works in a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, on a laptop or tablet. The sequence is RefSeqGene NG_007666.1 (human HRAS), the same in all four labs.
